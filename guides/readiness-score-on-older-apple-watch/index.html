How to Get a Readiness Score on an Older Apple Watch

Apple's Readiness score arrived with watchOS 27 — on two watches. If you wear anything older, here is what your Watch still does natively, why Apple's line sits where it does, and how to get a daily readiness score on a Series 4 through 11, an SE or an earlier Ultra.

By the Axon team · Published 14 October 2026 · Apple details checked 14 October 2026

Quick answer. Apple's Readiness app is available only on Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4. Series 9 through 11 can run watchOS 27 and still don't get it; older watches can't run watchOS 27 at all. To get a daily readiness score on an older Watch, use an app that reads the data your Watch already records. Axon does this on any Apple Watch Series 4 or later, SE or Ultra: it builds Recovery and Readiness on your iPhone from overnight heart rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep, compared with your own baseline. Plan on about a week of overnight wear before the numbers settle.

Which Apple Watches get Apple's Readiness score

Apple's guide lists the Readiness app for Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4, for people 13 and older. It arrived with watchOS 27 and the two new watches. Here is where every other Watch stands:

Axon does not depend on any of that: it reads the Health data your Watch has already saved to your iPhone, so it needs an iPhone on iOS 17 or later and a Watch that records overnight data (Series 4 or later, SE or Ultra).

Why Apple's line sits at the newest sensors

Series 12 and Ultra 4 carry Apple's new Health Sensing System. Apple says it measures heart rate every five seconds, all day, and heart rate variability (HRV) as often as every five minutes — 24 times more often than Series 11 does for HRV. Apple's Readiness score reads your daytime and overnight vitals against your recent baseline, so that denser stream is the obvious difference between the new watches and the rest. We can't see Apple's roadmap, so we make no prediction about whether that line moves.

What it means for an older Watch is plain: it records HRV far less often, so each night's number rests on fewer readings. A readiness score built on an older Watch is therefore a lighter read than Apple's, not a copy of it. The core idea still holds, because it is the same one Apple uses: compare each night with your own recent normal. A reading that is high or low for you is informative even when it is sparse. That is also why every scoring app, Axon included, needs a baseline before its numbers mean much.

Four ways to get a readiness score on an older Watch

1. Use what your Watch already shows

From Series 6 on, Sleep Score grades each night, and the Vitals app compares your overnight readings with your typical range. Both are useful, but neither combines into one call about today. Our guide to Sleep Score versus a recovery score covers the gap.

2. Add a scoring app that reads your Watch

An app such as Axon takes the HRV, resting heart rate and sleep your Watch has already recorded and turns them into Recovery and Readiness scores. Axon runs on any Series 4 or later, SE or Ultra, is free to use, and computes on your iPhone. Other apps do similar jobs; see our honest comparisons of Athlytic versus Axon and Bevel versus Axon.

3. Upgrade the Watch

If you were already going to replace your Watch, Apple's native Readiness is a fair reason to pick a Series 12 or Ultra 4: it lives on the wrist and uses the denser sensor data. If readiness is the only reason, try an app on the Watch you own first. A few weeks will show whether a daily number changes what you do.

4. Move to a ring, strap or sports watch

Oura, WHOOP, Garmin and others build their own scores on their own hardware, which means buying that hardware first. Our ranked list of WHOOP alternatives lays out the trade-offs, cost included.

Set up Axon on an older Watch

  1. Install Axon on an iPhone running iOS 17 or later. There is no account to create to start.
  2. Allow Health access when asked, so Axon can read the HRV, heart rate and sleep your Watch already collects.
  3. Wear the Watch to sleep. Recovery and Readiness are built from overnight data, so a night without the Watch is a night without a fresh reading.
  4. Check Home each morning. Sleep and Strain can show real numbers from day one. Recovery and Readiness show a building-baseline state until enough nights are in.

Axon computes your scores on your iPhone. Your health data never leaves your phone unless you say so, and Axon's usage analytics carry no health values.

What the first week looks like

For roughly seven nights, expect a building-baseline placeholder where Recovery and Readiness will go. That is Axon declining to invent a number before it knows your normal. Apple's own Readiness has the same run-up: its guide asks for at least seven nights wearing the Watch to sleep. After that, scores refine night by night, and respiratory rate keeps settling into its role over about 30 days. On a night the Watch wasn't worn, the readings it would have given show as a dash with the reason, never a stand-in number.

Who should not pick Axon for this

  • You are buying a Series 12 or Ultra 4 anyway and want Apple's built-in Readiness on the wrist. You don't need an app for the headline number.
  • You won't wear your Watch to sleep. Neither Apple's Readiness nor Axon's has a morning starting point without overnight data.
  • You want a coach that sets targets — a bedtime or an exertion ceiling. Axon shows scores and the reasons behind them and stops there; Athlytic does the coaching.
  • You don't own an Apple Watch. Without one, Axon can show partial Sleep and Strain but not Recovery or Readiness.

Does the Apple Watch Series 11 have a readiness score?

No. Apple's Readiness app is available only on Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4. A Series 11 can run watchOS 27 but does not get the app. It keeps Sleep Score and Vitals. An app such as Axon can add a daily readiness score to a Series 11 by reading its Health data.

Can I get Apple's Readiness score by updating to watchOS 27?

Only if your watch is a Series 12 or Ultra 4. Series 9 through 11, Ultra 2 and 3 and SE (3rd generation) can install watchOS 27 without gaining the Readiness app, and Series 6 through 8, SE (2nd generation) and the first Ultra can't install watchOS 27 at all.

Which Apple Watches work with Axon's Recovery and Readiness?

Axon needs an iPhone on iOS 17 or later paired with an Apple Watch Series 4 or later, an SE or an Ultra. Some readings, such as blood oxygen and wrist temperature, depend on sensors only newer models have. Where a sensor is missing, that row shows as unavailable rather than a made-up value.

Is a readiness score from an older Apple Watch as good as Apple's?

It is a different, lighter read. Apple's Series 12 and Ultra 4 record HRV far more often (Apple says 24 times as often as Series 11), so Apple's score rests on more data. Axon compares each night on an older Watch with your own baseline, which still shows whether you are above or below your usual. Both are wellness estimates, not a diagnosis.

How long until a readiness score shows up?

Apple says its Readiness needs at least seven nights of wearing the Watch to sleep. Axon needs a similar run-up: about a week of overnight wear before Recovery and Readiness move past a building-baseline placeholder, with respiratory rate settling over about 30 days.
