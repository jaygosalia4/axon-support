Body Battery on Apple Watch: Is There an Equivalent?

If you are coming from Garmin, you probably miss one number: the energy gauge that falls through the day and refills overnight. Here is what Apple Watch has, what it doesn't, and how to get that kind of gauge from the Watch you already wear.

By the Axon team · Published 14 October 2026 · Garmin and Apple details checked 14 October 2026

Quick answer. Body Battery is Garmin's name for its reserve-energy gauge, and Apple Watch has no feature by that name. The nearest native feature is Apple's Readiness app, which updates through the day but runs only on Series 12 and Ultra 4. On any other Apple Watch, an app has to build the battery. Axon's Readiness works that way: a morning starting level from last night's sleep, recovery and recent load, which drains as your heart rate runs high and recharges as it settles, with naps adding charge back.

What Garmin's Body Battery is

Garmin's owner's manual says the watch analyzes your heart rate variability, stress level, sleep quality and activity to set your level, and likens it to a car's gas gauge: the reserve energy you have available. A few specifics from the manual:

  • Scale. The English vívoactive 5 manual gives 5 to 100 (5–25 very low, 26–50 low, 51–75 medium, 76–100 high). Other Garmin manuals differ slightly by model.
  • Recharge. Good sleep charges it, and Garmin advises wearing the watch overnight for more accurate results.
  • Drain. Strenuous activity and high stress drain it faster.
  • What doesn't count. Garmin says food intake and stimulants such as caffeine have no impact.
  • On the watch. A glance shows the current level, and a graph shows the day since midnight.
A note on the name. Body Battery is a Garmin trademark. Axon is independent and not affiliated with Garmin; we use the name only because it is what people search for.

Does Apple Watch have Body Battery?

No. Apple Watch has no feature by that name. The closest thing Apple offers is the Readiness app that arrived with watchOS 27 — on Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 only. Apple describes it as a single 0 to 10 score with four labels (Recover, Pace Yourself, Ready, Go For It), built from recent activity, vitals and sleep, and its guide says the score updates through the day with calories burned, naps and daytime heart rate.

Everything else on the Watch is a separate piece. Sleep Score grades one night. Vitals compares your overnight readings with your typical range. Useful, but neither gives you a level that falls and refills through the day. If you own a Series 11 or older, there is no native readiness score at all; our guide to getting a readiness score on an older Apple Watch has the full model-by-model picture.

How a battery-style score is built from Apple Watch data

Every battery-style score has the same three parts: a starting level from how well you recovered, a drain from effort and stress, and a recharge from rest. Garmin builds its version inside its own watches. On an Apple Watch the raw material already sits in Apple Health — overnight HRV, resting heart rate, sleep stages and heart rate through the day — and an app supplies the model.

How Axon's Readiness works as a battery

  • Starting level (the morning anchor). When you wake, Axon sets the anchor from last night's Sleep score (40%), your Recovery score (30%) and Activity Balance (30%), which weighs the load you have been carrying against your recent fitness. If there's no sleep record for the night, Sleep is left out instead of scored as zero, and Recovery and Activity Balance count for half each.
  • Drain. Readiness falls while your heart rate runs above your personal relaxed band, faster the harder you push. A stress response — a raised resting-band heart rate with suppressed HRV — can drain it without a workout.
  • Recharge. It refills when your heart rate sits at or below that band. A nap your Watch detects, or one you log, adds charge back.
  • Ceiling and floor. It never rises above where you woke, and it has no floor: a hard day can take it to single digits.

For example, a morning anchor of 78 might read 40 after a demanding afternoon and climb back some by evening, but never past 78. The useful habit is to notice the shape of the day, not only the number you woke up with.

What to expect from a battery on Apple Watch

It won't match your old number. Axon's Readiness uses different inputs, a different scale and different math from Body Battery. Compare your own days with each other, not across brands.

It is as fresh as your Health data. The battery is only as current as the heart-rate data your Watch has saved to Apple Health and Axon has read. Axon refreshes from Health when you open it, and its widgets and Watch complication show the most recent value the app has stored.

Missing data stays missing. If a reading is absent — the Watch wasn't worn overnight, say — Axon shows a dash and the reason for that reading instead of a guess, and never scores the missing part as zero. For the first week, Recovery and Readiness show a building-baseline state while Axon learns your normal.

It's a wellness estimate. Axon's scores don't diagnose anything and aren't a substitute for a doctor. Axon computes them on your iPhone, and your health data never leaves your phone unless you say so.

Who should not pick Axon for this

  • You're happy on a Garmin watch. Body Battery is built in, and Axon adds nothing you need.
  • You want the same numbers you had before. No other app will reproduce Garmin's model, and Axon doesn't try to.
  • You won't wear your Apple Watch to sleep. The morning level has little to start from without overnight data.
  • You want instructions, not a gauge. Axon shows the score and the reasons behind it; it doesn't set a bedtime or an exertion target. Athlytic does.

Does Apple Watch have Body Battery?

No. Body Battery is Garmin's feature. Apple's nearest equivalent is the Readiness app on Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, a 0 to 10 score that updates through the day. On every earlier Apple Watch you need an app that builds a battery-style score from your Watch's Health data.

What is the Apple Watch equivalent of Garmin Body Battery?

Apple's Readiness score on Series 12 and Ultra 4 is the closest native feature. For other Apple Watches, Axon's Readiness works as a live battery: it starts each morning from your Sleep, Recovery and Activity Balance, drains when your heart rate runs above your relaxed band, and recharges at rest.

Is Axon's Readiness the same as Body Battery?

No. Body Battery is Garmin's own model, on a 5 to 100 scale in Garmin's vívoactive 5 manual. Axon's Readiness is a separate model on a 0 to 100 scale, built from your Apple Watch data, so the numbers won't match. Axon is independent and not affiliated with Garmin.

Does Apple's Readiness score change during the day?

Yes. Apple's guide says the score updates throughout the day based on calories burned, naps and your daytime heart rate. It is available only on Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4.

Do I need to wear my Apple Watch to sleep to get a battery score?

Yes, for the morning starting level. Axon's anchor is built from last night's sleep and overnight recovery signals. If the Watch wasn't worn, the readings it would have given show as a dash with the reason rather than a guessed number.
